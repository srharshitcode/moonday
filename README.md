## Part I: The Story Every Woman Knows (Vision, Empathy, and Real Life)

### 1. The Story Nobody Writes Down: From the School Hallway to Late Night Quiet

Every woman carries a story in her body that she rarely tells out loud.

It almost always begins when she is still a child. Maybe she is twelve or thirteen, sitting at a wooden desk in a warm classroom while the teacher writes on the board. Halfway through the lesson, she feels a strange, heavy pulling sensation low in her stomach that she has never felt before. When the bell rings, she walks into the school washroom, locks the stall door, and sees a rust colored stain on her clothes. In that single second, a wave of panic, confusion, and heat rushes to her face. She folds a piece of tissue, tucks a pad up the sleeve of her sweater so nobody in the hallway notices, and walks back to her desk pretending everything is completely normal.

Right there, on that first afternoon, she learns the unwritten rule that the world hands to almost every girl: *Whatever is happening inside your body, keep it hidden, keep smiling, and never let it slow you down.*

That rule follows her through every chapter of her life. It sits beside her on school buses when she is afraid to stand up and check the back of her skirt. It follows her into sports practice when her legs feel like lead. It follows her into adulthood, where she is expected to show up with the exact same energy on the first day of a heavy period as she does on her brightest day of the month.

MoonDay was born out of a simple realization. Women have spent generations adapting themselves to a world that refuses to acknowledge their biology. We wanted to build a quiet, safe corner of the internet that adapts to *her* instead.

### 2. Growing Up Whispering: How Young Girls Learn to Hide Their Pain

Think about how young girls talk about their periods when they are growing up. They pass pads under desks like contraband. They whisper code words in hallways. When a girl is doubled over with cramps in gym class or sitting quietly at the family dinner table because she feels nauseous, she is often told that she is being dramatic or that "every woman goes through it, so just get used to it."

When a young girl hears that message enough times, she starts doubting her own senses. She stops paying attention to what her body is trying to tell her. Instead of learning that her menstrual cycle is a natural rhythm of hormones that affects her sleep, her appetite, her focus, and her mood, she learns to treat her body like an inconvenience that betrays her once a month.

Even worse, nobody teaches her what is actually normal and what deserves medical attention. She does not know why her period is irregular during her first three years of puberty. She does not know why her lower back aches, or why she gets a headache the day before she bleeds, or why certain weeks of the month make schoolwork feel effortless while other weeks make concentration feel impossible.

We built MoonDay so that a young girl or woman opening this app feels an immediate sense of relief. Nothing here is whispered or treated as shameful. Every symptom has a real name, every phase has a clear explanation, and every feeling is met with warmth.

### 3. The College and Early Career Years: Carrying Cramps Through Exams and Shifts

As a girl steps into her late teens and twenties, life speeds up, and the pressure to ignore her body grows even heavier.

Picture her at nineteen, sitting in a freezing lecture hall for a three hour final exam. Her period started four hours ago. Her lower spine feels like it is locked in a vise, a dull migraine is pressing behind her left eye, and waves of prostaglandin nausea keep rolling through her stomach. Yet the exam clock does not care what day of her cycle it is. She takes two painkillers with lukewarm water, rests her forehead on her hand, and forces herself to finish the paper.

Picture her at twenty three, standing in front of her mirror twenty minutes before leaving for work or class. Three days ago, her clothes fit comfortably. This morning, late luteal water retention has left her stomach bloated and her breasts tender. She tries on three different outfits, feels a sudden wave of frustration and self consciousness, and finally pulls on an oversized sweater just to feel hidden.

Picture her at twenty seven, working a ten hour shift on her feet at a hospital, teaching a room full of students, or sitting through back to back client calls. Someone asks how her week is going. She smiles and says, *"All good, just a little tired!"* while underneath the desk she is pressing her palm against her lower abdomen, counting the hours until she can get home, plug in her heating pad, and curl up under a blanket.

Every woman knows this quiet bravery. She carries real physical pain and hormonal exhaustion through ordinary Tuesdays and Wednesdays without ever asking for credit. MoonDay was designed to be the place where she finally gets to set that weight down.

### 4. The 2:30 AM Question: Why Do I Feel Like a Different Person Tonight?

Physical pain is only half of the story. For millions of women, the hardest part of the monthly cycle happens in the quiet of their own minds during the four or five days before their period arrives.

Imagine a woman lying awake at 2:30 in the morning. The house is completely still. Just four days ago, she felt capable, optimistic, and secure in her relationships. Tonight, for no obvious reason she can point to, her chest feels tight with dread. Her mind is replaying awkward conversations from years ago. A slightly short text message from her partner or best friend earlier that evening has convinced her that the people she loves are pulling away from her. Tears slip sideways down her temples into her pillow, and she whispers the question that almost every woman has asked the ceiling in the dark:

*"What is wrong with me? Why am I falling apart over nothing?"*

She is not falling apart, and it is not over nothing. Inside her brain, the sudden late luteal withdrawal of progesterone and its calming metabolite, allopregnanolone, has temporarily deprived her $\text{GABA}_A$ receptors of their natural brake pedal, while dipping estradiol has lowered her available serotonin. Her nervous system is literally experiencing a sudden neurochemical withdrawal.

Forty eight hours later, her period starts. Within a day, the heavy dark curtain in her mind lifts. She wakes up, looks out the window, and feels like herself again.

Yet if she does not have a private, trustworthy place to track those emotional shifts alongside her cycle dates, the next month she will forget that it was Day 26 talking. When day 26 rolls around again, she will blame herself all over again. MoonDay breaks that cycle of self blame forever. By showing her the exact link between her calendar and her emotional weather, MoonDay turns *"What is wrong with me?"* into *"It is Day 26. My hormones are dipping. I am safe, this feeling is temporary, and I just need to be gentle with myself tonight."*

### 5. The Exhaustion of Living in a 24 Hour World With a Monthly Body

Have you ever wondered why some weeks you can wake up early, finish your work ahead of schedule, cook a healthy dinner, and still want to talk to friends, while two weeks later the exact same daily routine feels like climbing a mountain with a heavy backpack?

Society teaches us that discipline means doing the exact same thing at the exact same intensity every single day of the year. Wake up at 6:00 AM, drink the same coffee, do the same intense workout, eat the same number of calories, and socialize at the same pace, 365 days a year.

When women try to force their bodies into that rigid daily mold, they end up burning out during the second half of every month. Then they blame themselves for lacking willpower.

The truth is not a lack of willpower. The truth is that your body was never designed to run on a flat line. Living inside a female body means living inside a cyclical tide, and learning to read that tide is one of the greatest gifts a woman can give herself.

### 6. How the Male Circadian Clock Shaped Modern Work and Fitness

To understand why the modern schedule feels so exhausting during certain weeks of your cycle, it helps to look at how the standard workday and fitness routines were invented.

In male biology, sex hormones follow a single $24\text{ hour}$ circadian rhythm. Testosterone peaks in the early morning, supports physical drive and focus through the middle of the day, declines gradually in the evening, and resets during sleep. Because this $24\text{ hour}$ loop repeats identically every day, a man's hormonal baseline on Day 5 of the month is essentially identical to his hormonal baseline on Day 24 of the month.

Historically, almost all medical research, workplace productivity models, and athletic training programs were tested on male subjects specifically because researchers considered the female menstrual cycle "too variable" to include in clinical trials. As a result, the world adopted a $24\text{ hour}$ template as the universal human standard.

When a woman follows a training plan or work schedule built solely for a $24\text{ hour}$ hormone loop, the schedule matches her biology during her follicular and ovulatory weeks, when her estrogen is rising. However, during her late luteal and menstrual days, when her body is prioritizing tissue repair, higher basal body temperature, and nervous system protection, that same rigid schedule fights directly against her physiology.

### 7. Understanding the Infradian Rhythm: Your Second Internal Clock

Women have two biological clocks running at the same time. The first is the $24\text{ hour}$ circadian clock that governs waking and sleeping. The second is the **infradian rhythm**, a monthly biological cycle lasting roughly $25$ to $35\text{ days}$ that governs reproduction, metabolism, immunity, brain chemistry, and stress response.

Across this monthly rhythm, four key hormones rise and fall in a coordinated sequence:
* **Follicle Stimulating Hormone ($\text{FSH}$)** secreted by the anterior pituitary gland to mature ovarian follicles.
* **Luteinizing Hormone ($\text{LH}$)** secreted in a sharp mid cycle surge to trigger ovulation.
* **Estradiol ($E_2$)**, the primary form of estrogen, which builds the uterine lining and energizes the brain, muscles, and metabolism.
* **Progesterone ($P_4$)**, the thermogenic hormone of the second half of the cycle, which stabilizes the uterine lining, raises core body temperature by $0.2^\circ\text{C}$ to $0.5^\circ\text{C}$, and calms the central nervous system.

Because hormone receptors for $E_2$ and $P_4$ are distributed throughout the entire body, your infradian rhythm touches everything from how fast your gut digests food to how sensitive your eyes and ears are to bright lights and loud noises. MoonDay is built to help you live in harmony with this second clock rather than fighting against it.

### 9. The Hidden Economy of Menstrual Surveillance and Ad Brokers

Over the past several years, privacy watchdogs and consumer protection investigations uncovered a troubling reality across the femtech industry. Many popular free period trackers were monetizing their users by embedding third party marketing software development kits (SDKs) directly inside their apps.

Whenever a user logged her period dates, her mood swings, her pregnancy tests, or her vaginal symptoms, those events were quietly packaged into behavioral metadata and transmitted to advertising networks and data brokers. Women who logged late periods or ovulation symptoms suddenly saw their social media feeds flooded with targeted ads for baby products, fertility clinics, or supplements before they had even spoken to their own families.

Health data is the most intimate information a human being generates. Turning a woman's pain, cycle dates, and private emotional notes into a product for advertisers is a betrayal of trust. MoonDay was built with zero third party advertising trackers, zero data brokers, and zero behavioral profiling.

### 12. Why Your Cycle Is the Fifth Vital Sign of Your Health

When you go for a medical checkup, clinicians measure four classic vital signs: body temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, and blood pressure. Leading medical bodies, including the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), recognize the **menstrual cycle as the Fifth Vital Sign** of female health.

Why does your cycle carry the same clinical weight as your heart rate or blood pressure?

Because a regular, healthy menstrual cycle is not just about your uterus. It is a monthly full body stress test. For your body to mature an egg, ovulate on time, build a healthy uterine lining, and shed it smoothly without debilitating pain, your brain, thyroid, adrenal glands, liver, gut, and ovaries all have to work in harmony. When something in your life or health falls out of balance, your period is almost always the very first system to let you know.

### 13. How the Brain and Ovaries Talk: The HPO Axis Explained Simply

Behind every calendar date in MoonDay is a continuous conversation between your brain and your ovaries called the **Hypothalamic Pituitary Ovarian ($\text{HPO}$) Axis**:

$$
\text{Hypothalamus (GnRH Pulses)} \longrightarrow \text{Anterior Pituitary (FSH \& LH)} \longrightarrow \text{Ovaries (Estradiol } E_2 \text{ \& Progesterone } P_4\text{)} \longrightarrow \text{Endometrium}
$$

Here is how that conversation works in plain language:
1. Deep in your brain, the **hypothalamus** acts like an air traffic controller. It constantly monitors your stress levels (cortisol), sleep, nutrition, and energy reserves. When it senses that your body is safe and well nourished, it sends out tiny rhythmic pulses of Gonadotropin Releasing Hormone ($\text{GnRH}$).
2. Those pulses tell the **pituitary gland** to release Follicle Stimulating Hormone ($\text{FSH}$), which travels through your bloodstream to your ovaries and wakes up a group of follicles containing eggs.
3. As the leading follicle grows, it releases **estradiol ($E_2$)**. Once estradiol reaches a high threshold, it signals the brain to release a surge of Luteinizing Hormone ($\text{LH}$), which causes the ovary to release the egg (**ovulation**).
4. After the egg is released, the empty follicle turns into a temporary hormone gland called the *corpus luteum*, which produces **progesterone ($P_4$)** for roughly $12$ to $16\text{ days}$. If no pregnancy occurs, progesterone and estrogen fall, triggering your period and starting the conversation all over again.

If you experience high stress, travel across time zones, catch a virus, or undereat during the first half of your cycle, your hypothalamus can pause those $\text{GnRH}$ pulses to protect you, delaying ovulation and making your period arrive late. Tracking in MoonDay helps you see this brain to body connection in real time.

## Part II: Your Four Inner Seasons (Living in Harmony With Your Biology)

### 15. Your Four Inner Seasons: Learning to Stop Fighting Yourself

Nature does not bloom twelve months a year. A tree rests in winter, buds in spring, opens fully in summer, and turns inward in autumn.

Your body moves through those exact same four inner seasons every single cycle. When you understand which season you are standing in today, you stop fighting your natural energy levels and start working with them.

### 16. Season One: The Menstrual Phase (Inner Winter, Letting Go and Resting)

Your **Menstrual Phase** begins on Day 1 of true red bleeding and typically lasts between $3$ and $7\text{ days}$.

In this inner winter, both estradiol ($E_2$) and progesterone ($P_4$) drop to their lowest baseline levels of the month. Because your body is actively shedding the endometrial lining and rebuilding its iron and fluid reserves, your physical battery naturally dips. It is completely normal to log `Fatigue`, `Low energy`, `Sleepiness`, or `Heavy-feeling pelvis` during these days.

At the same time, neuroimaging studies show that when both sex hormones are low during menstruation, connectivity between the left analytical hemisphere and the right intuitive hemisphere of your brain is remarkably strong. Many women find that once their initial cramps ease, their inner winter brings a quiet mental clarity about what is working in their lives and what they are ready to let go of.

### 17. How Prostaglandins and Iron Shifts Shape Your Period Days

Why do the first two days of your period often bring cramps, backaches, and sudden digestive changes?

As progesterone drops at the end of your previous cycle, the cells of your uterine lining release lipid compounds called **prostaglandins** (primarily $\text{PGF}_{2\alpha}$ and $\text{PGE}_2$). Prostaglandins have a specific job: they trigger the smooth muscle wall of the uterus (the myometrium) to contract so it can shed the old lining and pinch off tiny blood vessels.

When prostaglandin levels are high:
1. Strong uterine contractions temporarily squeeze the tiny blood vessels supplying oxygen to the uterine muscle. That temporary lack of oxygen (ischemia) is what your nerves register as **menstrual cramping**.
2. Excess prostaglandins can diffuse into the surrounding pelvic area, irritating the bowel (`Diarrhea`, `Painful bowel movements`, `Stomach cramps`) and referring pain through the sacral nerves into your lower back and thighs (`Lower-back pain`, `Leg cramps`).
3. Meanwhile, shedding blood reduces your circulating iron stores (`Weakness`, `Dizziness`, `Fatigue`).

Knowing this biology explains why MoonDay's self care engine emphasizes continuous warmth (which dilates blood vessels and restores oxygen flow to the uterine muscle) alongside iron rich foods paired with vitamin C during your period.

### 18. Season Two: The Follicular Phase (Inner Spring, Fresh Energy and Clear Thinking)

Once your bleeding stops, you step into your **Follicular Phase**, your body's inner spring (typically Days $6$ to $13$, though its length varies naturally from cycle to cycle).

During this phase, your pituitary gland releases Follicle Stimulating Hormone ($\text{FSH}$), stimulating several ovarian follicles to grow. One dominant follicle takes the lead and begins secreting increasing amounts of **estradiol ($E_2$)** while progesterone remains low.

After the quiet heaviness of your period, this rising tide of estradiol feels like opening the curtains on a bright spring morning. Your physical stamina rebounds, your skin barrier hydrates and clears, and your outlook naturally shifts outward.

### 19. How Rising Estradiol Boosts Dopamine, Serotonin, and Focus

Estradiol does much more than thicken the lining of your uterus. When estradiol crosses the blood brain barrier during your follicular phase, it acts as a powerful neuroprotective and mood elevating signal:

* **Serotonin Boost**: Estradiol increases the synthesis of serotonin (your brain's contentment and mood stability neurotransmitter) and increases the number of serotonin receptors in the brain.
* **Dopamine and Motivation**: Estradiol enhances dopamine signaling, which drives motivation, curiosity, pleasure, and goal directed focus.
* **Insulin Sensitivity and Muscle Recovery**: Higher estrogen improves how efficiently your cells use glucose for fuel and helps muscles recover faster after exercise.

This is why women so often log moods like `Motivated`, `Inspired`, `Curious`, `Hopeful`, and `Happy` during their follicular week. It is a wonderful biological window for brainstorming, studying complex material, starting new projects, and enjoying higher energy movement.

### 20. Season Three: The Ovulatory Phase (Inner Summer, Strength, Voice, and Presence)

Your **Ovulatory Phase** is your inner summer, a short, vibrant $2$ to $4\text{ day}$ window in the middle of your cycle when your fertile window peaks and an egg is released.

In MoonDay, your ovulation day is calculated dynamically using your personal luteal phase length rather than a generic guess:

$$
D_{\text{ovulation}} = L_{\text{cycle}} - L_{\text{luteal}}
$$

For many women, inner summer brings the highest social and communicative energy of the entire month. Because estradiol is at its peak alongside a small mid cycle rise in testosterone, brain areas responsible for verbal fluency and social empathy are highly active. You may find it easier to speak up in meetings, have important conversations, connect with friends, and feel confident in your skin (`Confident`, `Joyful`, `Playful`, `Affectionate`).

### 21. The Biology of Ovulation: LH Surges, Cervical Fluid, and Peak Confidence

Physiologically, ovulation is the main event of the menstrual cycle:
1. When estradiol stays above a critical threshold forroughly $48\text{ hours}$, the brain switches from negative feedback to positive feedback, releasing a dramatic surge of **Luteinizing Hormone ($\text{LH}$)**.
2. About $24$ to $36\text{ hours}$ after the $\text{LH}$ surge begins, the dominant follicle ruptures and releases its egg into the fallopian tube.
3. Under peak estrogen, the crypts of your cervix produce clear, watery, or stretchy fluid (`Egg-white-like` and `Stretchy` discharge) that alkalinizes the vaginal environment and creates a supportive pathway for sperm.
4. About $1$ in $5$ women feel a distinct, brief twinge or ache on one side of their lower pelvis when the follicle releases the egg (`Ovary pain` or *mittelschmerz*), sometimes accompanied by a drop of light `Pinkish` or `Spotting` fluid as estrogen briefly dips right after ovulation.

### 22. Season Four: The Luteal Phase (Inner Autumn, Slowing Down and Protecting Your Peace)

Immediately after ovulation, you enter your **Luteal Phase**, your body's inner autumn, which lasts roughly $12$ to $16\text{ days}$ (averaging $14\text{ days}$) until your next period begins.

It helps to think of your luteal phase in two halves:
* **Early Luteal (First 6 to 7 Days)**: The ruptured follicle transforms into the *corpus luteum* and secretes a steady wave of **progesterone ($P_4$)** alongside a secondary rise in estrogen. Energy feels steady, calm, and detail oriented. It is a natural time for finishing tasks, organizing your home, and nesting.
* **Late Luteal (Final 5 to 7 Days)**: If the egg was not fertilized, the corpus luteum begins to break down. Both progesterone and estrogen drop rapidly toward baseline. This hormonal withdrawal is what triggers premenstrual symptoms (PMS).

### 23. Progesterone, Allopregnanolone, and the Late Luteal Caloric Burn

Progesterone changes your daily physiology in three major ways during the luteal phase:

1. **Higher Basal Body Temperature**: Progesterone acts on the thermoregulatory center of your hypothalamus, raising your resting body temperature by $0.2^\circ\text{C}$ to $0.5^\circ\text{C}$.
2. **Higher Metabolic Rate and Calorie Needs**: Because your body is warmer and working to maintain a thick, nutrient rich uterine lining, your resting energy expenditure increases by roughly $5\%$ to $10\%$, burning an extra $100$ to $300\text{ kcal/day}$. When you log `Increased appetite`, `Carbs`, or `Chocolate` in MoonDay during your luteal phase, your body is genuinely asking for fuel to match that higher burn rate.
3. **The Allopregnanolone Brain Shift**: Inside the brain, progesterone is converted into a neurosteroid called **allopregnanolone**, which binds to $\text{GABA}_A$ receptors and produces a soothing, anti anxiety effect. When progesterone drops sharply in the final days before your period, allopregnanolone drops with it, leaving your nervous system temporarily raw, easily overstimulated, and prone to `Anxiety`, `Irritability`, `Insomnia`, and `Mood swings`.

### 24. Why Premenstrual Days Demand Extra Kindness and Steady Blood Sugar

During the late luteal days, your cells also become slightly more insulin resistant under progesterone's influence, meaning your blood sugar can spike and crash faster if you skip meals or drink coffee on an empty stomach. And when blood sugar crashes during the luteal phase, your adrenal glands release cortisol and adrenaline to bring your glucose back up, which feels inside your body like sudden shakiness, `Anxious` or `Panicky` feelings, `Headache`, and `Irritability`.

That is why MoonDay's luteal care guides encourage eating complex carbohydrates paired with protein every $3$ to $4\text{ hours}$, buffering caffeine with food, turning down harsh lights and noise in the evening, and treating yourself with extra patience.

Why does your stomach behave so differently in Week 3 compared to Day 1?

## Part VI: Cycle Mathematics, Clinical Reports, Education, and Privacy

### 68. How MoonDay Calculates Your Cycle Without Guessing

MoonDay combines your personal baseline settings with a dynamic historical averaging algorithm (`CalendarClient.tsx` and `MedicalReportClient.tsx`):

1. When you first join, MoonDay uses your configured baseline **Cycle length** (default $28\text{ days}$) and **Moonday length** (default $5\text{ days}$).
2. As you log `Confirmed Start Day` entries ($s_1, s_2, \dots, s_n$ sorted chronologically), MoonDay computes the inter-menstrual interval $\Delta_i$ between each pair of consecutive starts:

$$
\Delta_i = \text{differenceInDays}(s_{i+1}, s_i)
$$

3. To prevent an unlogged gap or outlier from skewing your predictions, MoonDay filters for physiologically valid intervals ($15 \le \Delta_i \le 60\text{ days}$) and calculates your rounded dynamic average:

$$
L_{\text{dynamic}} = \text{round}\left( \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=1}^{k} \Delta_j \right)
$$

### 69. Why Luteal Phase Math Beats the Old Day 14 Myth

Standard apps assume ovulation always occurs on Day 14 or at $L_{\text{cycle}} / 2$. Clinically, however, follicular phase length varies widely while the post-ovulatory **luteal phase ($L_{\text{luteal}}$)** remains relatively constant for each woman (typically $12$ to $16\text{ days}$, configurable in MoonDay Settings).

MoonDay calculates your expected ovulation day by subtracting your personal luteal phase length from your cycle length:

$$
D_{\text{ovulation}} = L_{\text{dynamic}} - L_{\text{luteal}}
$$

And constructs your **6 Day Fertile Window** across the five days leading up to ovulation (accounting for sperm survival in fertile cervical fluid) plus ovulation day itself:

$$
W_{\text{fertile}} = \left[ D_{\text{ovulation}} - 5, \; D_{\text{ovulation}} \right]
$$

In Tailwind CSS v4, utility classes take precedence over rules placed inside `@layer base`. Because many client views use explicit classes like `bg-white`, `bg-slate-50`, `bg-[#fff0f3]`, and `border-[#fbeaf0]`, we placed a **Global Dark Mode CSS Interceptor** at the root level of `src/app/globals.css` (outside `@layer base`).

Whenever `html.dark` is active on the document root, those selectors automatically remap light hex backgrounds, borders, and text colors across every component in the app to our Soft Aubergine dark palette without requiring hundreds of manual `dark:` classes in JSX.

### 83. Offline First Engineering: How IndexedDB Protects Every Keystroke

In `src/utils/offlineSync.ts` and `src/components/NetworkResilience.tsx`, we built a custom local draft engine using the browser's native **IndexedDB** (`moonday_offline_store`, version $3$):
* **Debounced Autosave (`triggerAutosave`)**: Every chip tap or text change starts a $1000\text{ ms}$ debounce timer.
* **Local Draft Persistence (`saveLocalDraft`)**: Writes an `OfflineDraft` object to the `drafts` object store before attempting any network request.
* **Offline Queueing (`offline-queued`)**: If `navigator.onLine` is `false`, MoonDay keeps the draft safely queued on your device and displays a clear amber banner.
* **Automatic Reconnection Sync (`flushPendingDrafts`)**: As soon as the browser fires the `online` event, MoonDay iterates through all unsynced drafts in IndexedDB and syncs them to Supabase automatically.

### 84. Solving Sync Conflicts, PWA Setup, Local Installation, and Vercel Deployment

#### Self Conflict Prevention in `NetworkResilience.tsx`
When an autosave completes, `syncDraftToSupabase` returns the new `serverUpdatedAt: nowIso` timestamp, which updates `latestBaseUpdatedAtRef.current`. This ensures that clicking the main **Save MoonDay Log** button a few seconds after an autosave never mistakes your own autosave for a conflict from another device.

#### Progressive Web App (PWA) Configuration
In `next.config.ts`, `@ducanh2912/next-pwa` is configured with `reloadOnOnline: false` so reconnecting to Wi-Fi never triggers a forced page reload while you are typing.
